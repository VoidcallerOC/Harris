Care sheet

Corn snake

Pantherophis guttatus. Starting notes from the Harris reptiles pages. This is not a complete manual — buy a book, find a reptile vet before you need one, and ask Adam or Ashlee about the animal on the rack.

Hatchlings

Hardy. Feed an appropriately sized rodent — often a little larger than the snake’s head. Pinkies for babies. Vitamins can go on the prey; many young snakes refuse food that smells unfamiliar, so dust the rear of the mouse if needed. Twice a week for the first few months, then weekly. Small water bowl, cleaned often.

Adults

Larger prey, every 7 to 10 days in the warm months. Less vitamin dusting. Many adults do better with water offered a couple of times a week, then the bowl removed so they do not soak it or foul it.

General

Southeastern U.S., dusk and dawn. A 10-gallon works for a baby; adults want at least a 30-gallon or the same footprint — the snake should stretch along the long side. Shoe-box tubs are fine for hatchlings. Paper or newspaper is sanitary; the shop uses and recommends shredded aspen so they can burrow. Hide on the cool end. Warm end 85–90°F. Tight, clip-on lid. They will find any gap.

Always buy a healthy captive-bred animal. Ask age, sex, and what it is eating. These sheets get you started and are by no means complete.
