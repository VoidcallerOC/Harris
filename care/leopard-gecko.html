Care sheet

Leopard gecko

Eublepharis macularius. Starting notes from the Harris reptiles pages. This is not a complete manual — buy a book, find a reptile vet before you need one, and ask Adam or Ashlee about the animal on the rack.

Hatchlings

Hardier than most baby lizards. Crickets no longer than half the width of the head (about 1/8 to 1/4 inch). Gut-load the crickets; dust with vitamin/mineral powder. Feed every day or two. Mealworms, waxworms, butterworms only as variety — they are fat or chitin. Small water bowl, cleaned.

Adults

Larger prey two to three times a week. Less dusting. An occasional pinkie is useful, especially for breeding females.

General

Dry rocky plains of Afghanistan, Pakistan, northwest India. Crepuscular. A 10-gallon holds an adult or a pair. Paper for babies so they do not eat sand; adults can go on sand or calci-sand. Hide box. Heat pad or controlled heat rock on one end. They do not care about fancy lights; they do care about a retreat from heat.

Always buy a healthy captive-bred animal. Ask age, sex, and what it is eating. These sheets get you started and are by no means complete.
