Care sheet

White’s treefrog

Litoria caerulea. Starting notes from the Harris reptiles pages. This is not a complete manual — buy a book, find a reptile vet before you need one, and ask Adam or Ashlee about the animal on the rack.

General

Large, easy-going, and the shop’s beginner frog. They tolerate careful handling if your hands are free of soap, detergent, and bug spray. Captive-bred babies are the ones to buy; blue phases exist. Crickets about 1/8 to 1/4 inch, gut-loaded and dusted. Young frogs every other day. Waxworms, butterworms, moths, roaches as variety, worms sparingly. Medium water bowl, cleaned.

Adults

Larger prey two to three times a week. Occasional pinkie. A 10-gallon holds an adult or a pair; they want height to climb. Paper for juveniles. Adults can go on large pea gravel. Keep humidity on the low side — a large water bowl, not a fog jungle — or you get ammonia and red-leg. Coil or fluorescent lighting is enough.

Always buy a healthy captive-bred animal. Ask age, sex, and what it is eating. These sheets get you started and are by no means complete.
