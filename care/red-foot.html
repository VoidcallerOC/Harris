Care sheet

Red-foot tortoise

Chelonoidis carbonaria. The old Harris reptiles page recommended red-foots for beginners but did not publish a full sheet. This is that chooser copy, not a 2015 price list.

From the shop chooser

Turtles and tortoises are active, personable, hardy, interesting — and voracious, messy, and long-lived. Land tortoises get large, eat every day, and may live well over a hundred years. Be honest about the maintenance before you take one home. For beginners they named red-foots, Russian tortoises, and red-eared sliders.

What to do here

Do not copy a 2015 price or a generic internet setup. Ask Adam or Ashlee for the enclosure, heat, humidity, and diet that matches the animal on the rack this week. Bring photos of your space. If you cannot keep up with daily food and a wet, planted area, pick a different beginner.

Captive-bred

When they have red-foots they have been captive-bred juveniles. That is the animal to buy — not a wild one that has already been shipped and stressed.

Always buy a healthy captive-bred animal. Ask age, sex, and what it is eating. These sheets get you started and are by no means complete.
