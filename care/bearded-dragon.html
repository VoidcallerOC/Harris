Care sheet

Bearded dragon

Pogona vitticeps. Starting notes from the Harris reptiles pages. This is not a complete manual — buy a book, find a reptile vet before you need one, and ask Adam or Ashlee about the animal on the rack.

Hatchlings

More delicate than a leopard gecko. Prey too large kills quietly — fine one day, listless the next. Crickets ¼ inch or smaller, no longer than half the head width. When in doubt, go smaller. Gut-load and dust. Feed every day. Mist daily; babies often ignore a water bowl. Some will take greens early, some only take what moves.

Adults

Eighteen to twenty-four inches, active, hungry. Bigger cage is better. Large crickets, superworms, pinkies, more vegetables, some fruit. Four to six days a week. Eight to ten years or more with light and food that actually match a desert lizard.

General

Australian desert. Day-active. 10-gallon for a baby for maybe eight months; a pair of adults wants about a 55-gallon. Paper for babies. Adults can use sand. Hide. The shop prefers a heat bulb in a reflector dome over a heat rock, plus a real UVB lamp so they can metabolize calcium. Do not cheap out on the lighting.

Always buy a healthy captive-bred animal. Ask age, sex, and what it is eating. These sheets get you started and are by no means complete.
