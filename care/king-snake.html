Care sheet

King snake

Lampropeltis species. Starting notes from the Harris reptiles pages. This is not a complete manual — buy a book, find a reptile vet before you need one, and ask Adam or Ashlee about the animal on the rack.

Hatchlings

Most kings are hardy. Pinkie or newborn mice, sized a little larger than the head. Same vitamin caution as corns. Twice a week at first, then weekly. Clean water bowl.

Adults

Larger prey every 7 to 10 days. Water a couple of times a week, then pull the bowl. They will spill it or defecate in it.

General

A large U.S. group — California, Mexican black, eastern chain, Florida, and the mountain kings as you get more advanced. Diurnal and crepuscular. Many kings eat other snakes: do not double them up, and be careful if you handle more than one. Same caging as corns: 10-gallon baby, 30-gallon adult, aspen, hide on the cool end, 85–90°F warm end, escape-proof lid.

Shop ladder

Beginners: California, Mexican black, eastern chain, Florida. Moderate: variable (thayeri), Mexican. Advanced: grey-banded, Arizona mountain. Differences are mostly feeding and temperament — ask before you pick a locality.

Always buy a healthy captive-bred animal. Ask age, sex, and what it is eating. These sheets get you started and are by no means complete.
