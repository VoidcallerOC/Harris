The living inventory

Not a big-box pet aisle.

Walk-ins describe it as a small-scale zoo: morphs you will not find down the road, feeders priced for keepers who actually feed, and staff who will talk you out of the wrong animal.

Snakes

Ball pythons, corns, hognose, milks, boas, and unusual colubrids.

Lizards & chelonians

Bearded dragons, geckos, skinks, tortoises, and turtles.

Amphibians

Dart frogs, pacman frogs, and other damp-room specialists.

Fish & gliders

Freshwater, marine, corals, and sugar gliders when available.

Why keepers drive here

Husbandry first. Sales second.

Adam Harris has been keeping and breeding reptiles for more than twenty years, with field time in Costa Rica, Thailand, Australia, South Africa, and the Bahamas. Seth Harris built the original shop as a fish-keeper and taught high-school biology for 31 years.

Many animals on the rack are captive-bred here or by local breeders the shop trusts. Feeders — pinkies through rats, crickets, roaches — are a reason regulars make the trip even when they are not adding a new animal.

Honest setups

Full retail on enclosures, heat, UV, substrate, live plants, and foods. They will size the feeder and the tub before they size the receipt.

Rare on the wall

Reviewers keep mentioning animals they have never seen at chain stores — morphs, odd colubrids, well-kept giants.

Programs & parties

Educational visits and birthday events that actually handle the animals. Book ahead. These fill.

The Harris story

From a house full of tanks to Route 44.

1940s · West Hartford

Seth starts with fish

The original Harris in Wonderland grew out of Seth Harris’s breeding hobby, then a small storefront on New Britain Avenue before a teaching career and military service closed the first chapter.

1999 · Bloomfield

Reopened next to a bar

Four hundred square feet, then 1,800 next door once the smoke and the crowding got old. Six years of building the modern shop.

Avon years

Two village spaces

Old Avon Village, then street level a quarter-mile away. The collection outgrew both.

Now · Canton

364 Albany Turnpike

Road frontage, dedicated parking, accessible entry, and room to be a proper fish-and-reptile specialty shop again — including pond work.
